# Cleanup Pass

Paste this as its own message after a draft exists (a fresh chat works best), then paste the draft under it.

You are editing a draft written in the author's voice for their blog. Remove the habits that make writing read as machine-made. This is a removal-only edit.

## You may
- Delete a word, a phrase, or a whole sentence.
- Swap a fancy word for a plain one ("utilize" becomes "use", "serves as" becomes "is").
- Fix spelling.
- Join a run of short, clipped sentences or fragments into one flowing sentence with a comma, the author's hyphen dash or an ellipsis, adding at most a joining "so" or "because".

## You may not
- Add sentences, headings, lists, transitions or a summary (beyond the joining word above).
- Change any fact, number, name, quote, code or link.
- Tidy up the author's voice (see "Leave these alone").

If a sentence only existed to sound impressive, delete it and leave the gap. Don't write a replacement.

## Remove
1. Puffed-up significance: "serves as a testament", "plays a pivotal role", "underscores the importance", "marks a shift", "in today's evolving landscape".
2. "-ing" tails bolted onto a sentence: "..., highlighting its versatility", "..., ensuring a smooth experience".
3. Brochure words: boasts, vibrant, nestled, showcasing, seamless, game-changer, cutting-edge.
4. Unnamed authorities: "experts say", "many users report", "it's widely believed". Name the source or cut it.
5. Summaries and morals: "In summary", "Overall", "Ultimately", a tidy last line that states the lesson, "The short version:" openers.
6. Throat-clearing: "It's important to note", "It's worth mentioning", "Let's dive in", "Here's a comprehensive breakdown".
7. Stock AI words: delve, crucial, robust, tapestry, intricate, meticulous, enhance, foster, leverage, realm, underscore, pivotal, landscape (the abstract kind).
8. Dodging "is" and "has": "stands as", "functions as", "represents a" become "is"; "features a", "offers a", "boasts a" become "has".
9. Setups: "It's not just X, it's Y", "not X, but Y", "no X, no Y, just Z".
10. Everything in threes: three adjectives or three parallel phrases, again and again. Keep the one or two that matter.
11. Synonym-hopping: "the tool... the utility... the solution" for one thing. Repeat the plain word.
12. Em dashes. Use the author's hyphen glued to the word before ("like this- and on"), a comma, or a period.
13. Punch-line paragraph endings and aphorisms: "Future you will thank you.", "And that changes everything."
14. Lists where every item starts with a bold label and a colon. One or two bold lead-ins at most.
15. Lists crammed into one sentence before a colon: "X, Y, Z, the ammo: any of these can..."
16. Stacks of abstract nouns: "rail-to-mount interface", "user engagement optimization". Say the plain thing.
17. Staccato: stacked fragments, verbless openers, clipped verdict lines ("Opus designs, Sonnet builds."), runs of short "X. Y. Z." sentences. Join them as allowed above; if a fragment adds nothing, delete it.

## Flag, don't delete
Any first-person claim about gear the author owns, money they spent, or things they tested, built, measured or broke. List them at the end under "Check these are real". Only the author knows.

## Leave these alone. They are the author's voice, not AI habits.
- Ellipses of any length (.. ... ....), including uneven ones.
- The hyphen dash glued to the word before: "like this- and on".
- Scare quotes around single words.
- Questions to the reader, and a "!" when the author is excited or annoyed.
- Repetition for emphasis: "many, many", "very, very".
- Casual asides and signposted detours: "I go off topic here...", "-OK way off topic!".
- Slash alternatives: "cable/ wire".
- Run-on sentences when the author is frustrated or excited.
- First-person lines about finding, sharing, remembering or being annoyed by the thing.
- The author undercutting themselves: "pray it works", "Not really.", "I strongly advise seeking a more thorough tutorial".
- Plain words: is, has, got, "same same", "a couple", slang, wordplay.
- A long rambling paragraph right next to a one-line one.
- The close: a question to readers, an "Oh, one more" afterthought, then the links.

## Output
The edited text only. Then a short list of what you removed, one line each, and the "Check these are real" list if there is one. No other commentary.
